# Data Science Mini-Project

**Predicting Post-Harvest Crop Loss in Nigeria Using the 2023 National Agricultural Sample Survey**

## Overview

Post-harvest crop loss (PHL) is a major threat to food security in Nigeria and impacts smallholder farmers’ livelihoods. In this project, the goal is to predict PHL in Nigeria using crop, farm, production, input-use, and household variables.

Question: How well can crop, household, and farm characteristics predict whether a household reports harvest or post-harvest loss for a crop during the main agricultural season?

## Modelling Approach

The project will apply binary classification to predict whether losses will occur during or after harvest, transport, and storage. Logistic regression will be used as the baseline model, and its performance will be compared to machine-learning models such as random forest and gradient-boosted classification models. Classification performance will be evaluated using precision, recall, and F1-score. If time permits, I will also try to predict the extent of crop loss where it happened.

## Dataset

The research will explore the 2023 Nigeria National Agricultural Sample Survey (NASS) dataset, which has 21 files that include household characteristics, plots, crops, agricultural inputs, production, and harvest results, with 334,133 records in the main-season harvest file.

## Research Focus

Previous studies on post-harvest loss mostly relied on statistical and econometric methods using older survey data. This project will explore the latest NASS 2023 dataset, published in 2025, and will focus on predictive modelling, comparison and validation of models, and identifying which variables contribute most to the model’s prediction of post-harvest crop loss.
